# Decisions

Every place the port departs from the original, or the BBC forces a choice
the original never had to make. Numbered, dated, with the reason. A decision
is only reversed by a new row that says why.

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-04 | **Screen: MODE 1 pixels, CRTC narrowed to 256 x 192** (64 columns x 24 rows, 12K at `&5000`) | The Spectrum's exact geometry, pixel for pixel, so the map and every graphic carry over unscaled. MODE 2's 8 colours would cost half the horizontal resolution (160 pixels can't hold 256), and the one-pixel ropes and brick patterns are the look of the game. MODE 1's pixels are close to the Spectrum's shape. 12K instead of 20K, and 512-byte rows make the address arithmetic cheap. How to fit the colours into four is decision 2, still open. |
| 2 | 2026-10-04 | **Colour: four colours per room**, chosen offline (`tools/mkrooms.py`): logical 0 is the room's background paper, the other three the colours covering the most pixels, and every Spectrum colour mapped to one of the four by brute force, keeping ink and paper distinct in every cell the room draws and every colour distinct from the background | Measured on the oracle's pixels: 98.8% of playfield pixels show in their own colour on average (worst room 91.4%). Changing the palette every character row would reach 99.95%, but needs an interrupt every 8 lines landing in a 64-cycle horizontal blank, and sprites would change colour from row to row. Revisit for the worst rooms if they look wrong in play. |
| 3 | 2026-10-04 | **Target: a stock BBC Model B** (32K, DFS). Sideways RAM, or a Master, only if it truly cannot fit, and then as a new row here | Matt's call: "try to fit this in a beeb first". The original is ~38K of code and data against ~19K free beside a 12K screen, so this shapes everything: room data packed and unpacked a room at a time, graphics kept 1bpp and coloured as they are drawn, front-end text off the critical path. |
| 4 | 2026-10-04 | **Text in the BBC's own font**, read from the MOS ROM at `&C000`, not the Spectrum ROM's | The Spectrum's text is its ROM font (`CHARS = &3C00`), which isn't ours to copy, and reading the MOS's costs no RAM. The room texts ("BEWARE OF THE DOG") and the status bar look a little different. |
| 5 | 2026-10-04 | **Rooms packed as bit streams**, one per room, unpacked into the original's bytecode when a room is entered (`tools/packrooms.py`, `src/unpack.6502`) | 13,633 bytes of room data become 7,770, saving 5.9K of a Model B (decision 3). LZ didn't see the structure: per-room ZX02 reached 10,220, 9,671 with a shared dictionary. The packing is a prefix code per command class, move-to-front lists for attribute, tile and type (they repeat from one record to the next ~80% of the time), and fixed widths for position and length, which are near-incompressible (4.2-5 bits of entropy each). Unpacking to the original bytes keeps the verified drawer unchanged. One run (room 33) gives column 32, which the original's arithmetic makes row 12 column 0; it is packed as that. |
| 6 | 2026-10-04 | **Yellow is one of every room's four colours** | Harry is yellow on the Spectrum in every room; with decision 2 alone room 1's palette had no yellow and he'd have been white there. Costs about a point: 97.8% of playfield pixels in their own colour on average (98.8% before), worst room 83% (room 5, whose white pipes turn yellow). |
| 7 | 2026-10-04 | **Keys are the Spectrum's for now**: Q up, A down, O left, P right, 1 take/drop, 0 abort, S save; SPACE jumps, standing in for SYMBOL SHIFT, which sits beside SPACE on a Spectrum | Matt: "the spectrum ones work and are authentic"; BBC-appropriate defaults are for later discussion (`docs/for-review.md`). Internal key numbers measured in jsbeeb (1 = 48, 0 = 39; Q = 16 and SPACE = 98 agree with the port kit's table). |
| 8 | 2026-10-04 | **Target revised: a BBC Model B with 16K of sideways RAM** (or a B+128, or a Master). Revises decision 3 | A stock Model B was tried first, as Matt asked, and the numbers say no: with the rooms packed there are 4.5K free under the screen, perhaps 6K with OS pages scavenged, and the rest of the game needs about 20K (Spectrum sizes: monster and machine graphics 7.5K, object sprites 1.3K, monster tables 1.2K, Harry 3.1K, monsters' code 2.7K, objects and machines' logic, sound, the in-play front end; `docs/memory-map.md`). The only stock-B route would be loading every room's data from disc as you enter it: pauses and drive noise on every screen. Sideways RAM was Matt's stated fallback; it is the port kit's baseline machine too. One bank holds the data and stays paged in, so the code reads it at `&8000` like any other memory. |
| 9 | 2026-10-04 | **Sprites drawn masked in their own colour, no attribute clash; collisions tested on the sprites' 1-bit images, not the screen** | The original ORs sprites on and recolours the ink of every cell they touch; the BBC can draw them over the scenery without disturbing its colours, which is what the original was after. Its collision test asks its 1-bit screen for pixels that are neither Harry's nor the room's; the BBC's 2-bit screen can't answer by colour (a black spider on black paper collides), so the same question is put to the sprites' images, masked by Harry's and the tiles' pixels. What that misses is only a hole one sprite's erase has punched in another. The erase itself is the original's: the room's pixels put back under the old image's own pixels only. |
| 10 | 2026-10-04 | **Decision 9 revised: collisions read the BBC's screen**, as the original reads its own | The image-based test missed the holes one sprite's erase leaves in another, and that changed which part of a pass Harry died in at room 2's dog. Since the sprites are drawn and erased exactly as the original does, in the same order, the BBC screen holds the same sprite pixels; a pixel counts if its colour isn't its cell's paper, less the tile's own pixels and Harry's. Blind spot: a sprite pixel in the colour of the paper under it (mostly the original's own invisible case, black on black). |
| 11 | 2026-10-04 | **No OS calls once the game is running**: the keyboard is read straight from the System VIA, and OS pages 1-3 hold tables | The machines layer overflowed main RAM by 32 bytes with sideways RAM full too. Owning the interrupts already kept the OS out of the frame; reading the keyboard matrix ourselves (as INKEY does) removes the last call, which frees `&0100-&017F`, `&0236-&02FF` and `&0300-&03FF` for constant tables copied in at start-up (532 bytes now). The cost: nothing in the game may call the OS again (sound included: the sound chip will be written directly). |
| 12 | 2026-10-04 | **Sound on the SN76489, written directly**: the life-lost tune on tone channel 0 (each note's divider and length measured from the original), the movement tick as two cycles of tone channel 1 at the original's pitch, the train's random beeper as white noise clocked by channel 2 at about 150 shifts a second | The original has only these three sounds, all beeper. Tones carry the tune and the tick exactly (pitch within a few percent for the tick, whose divider is computed rather than tabled). The beeper noise can't be copied sample for sample without a DAC trick that emulators may not reproduce; white noise at the same rate is the nearest robust sound. Timing comes from the User VIA's timer 2, polled, so the frame interrupt (and the status bar's palette split) keeps running through the tune. |
| 13 | 2026-10-04 | **The front end is a separate MODE 7 program, MENU, and the game returns to it by resetting the machine** | The original's front end (instructions, menu, high scores, redefine keys, save and load to tape) didn't fit beside the game (main RAM was down to about 240 bytes, sideways RAM to about 460), and saving and loading need the OS and the DFS, which the game has displaced. MENU runs with the OS alive and holds all of it: `!BOOT` runs it, it fills sideways RAM, shows the original's text where the original showed it, saves and loads one game, "CEGAME", to disc. The game hands back through a mailbox in the sideways bank (keys, why it stopped, the score, the high-score table, the RNG between games) and a state block at `&5F00` (zero page, LowState, the thing and monster tables), then resets: it puts back the OS bytes a soft BREAK keeps and flips start-up option bit 3 so the reset boots `!BOOT` without SHIFT, and MENU flips it back. A save in play goes to MENU, is written, and the game carries on from the block, as the original carries on after its tape save. Left out: the ZX Printer printout and "JOYSTICK COMPATIBLE" (no printer, no joystick yet); the © (teletext has none) is "(C)". Model B only for now: on a Master the reset doesn't come back to MENU. |
| 14 | 2026-10-04 | **The target is a BBC Model B with 16K of sideways RAM; the Master isn't one** (revises decision 8's "or a Master") | Matt asked for a Beeb first, with sideways RAM or a Master only at a push; the Beeb with sideways RAM works. The Master cost 512 bytes of sideways RAM for a copy of the font (its MOS doesn't keep the font at `&C000`), and its reset doesn't come back to MENU (decision 13 relies on OS 1.20's soft BREAK). Text now reads OS 1.20's font in its ROM at `&C000` directly (decision 4 otherwise stands), which frees the 512 bytes for the colour layer. |
| 15 | 2026-10-04 | **Palette bands: up to two splits a room, below which logical colour 2 and the colour map change** (Rich TW's and Matt's raster colours; refines decision 2) | Logical 0 (the paper) and 3 (Harry's yellow) can't change, and changing two colours at a split needs 8 ULA writes, too many for the 32us horizontal blank with interrupt jitter; one colour is 4 writes. With logical 1 fixed per room and logical 2 free per band, tools/mkrooms.py chooses the splits (kept only for a 0.2% gain in pixels shown in their own colour): 93 splits, 97.8% of pixels in their own colour becoming 99.0% (room 48: its cyan pipe and red bricks back). The drawing looks colours up by the cell's row, sprites per character row. T1 now runs free, latched for each split, so only VSync's latency moves the splits; measured clean from -20 to +4us on jsbeeb, set at -8. |
| 16 | 2026-10-04 | **The Master is supported again** (revises 14): on a Master the loader copies the MOS's font into HAZEL at `&C000`, and snapshots OS pages 2, 3 and `&0D` and zero page `&D0` there for the game's reset | Matt has only a Master to play on. MOS 3.20 keeps its font in bank 15, which can't be paged in while the data bank must stay at `&8000` (the interrupt reads the bands from it), so the font goes into HAZEL, the RAM that pages over the MOS's `&C000-&DFFF`. HAZEL then stays paged in for the game, which never calls the MOS (its IRQ entry is above `&E000`), and the game's font address doesn't change. MOS 3.20's soft BREAK trusts much more of the pages the game overwrites than OS 1.20's (the ROM table, the EXEC and SPOOL handles, the screen mode in `&0355`, the extended vectors under LowState), so `master_reset`, copied into HAZEL by the loader, puts them back before the reset. All in the loader, which is discarded: no main or sideways RAM. Checked on jsbeeb's Master (DFS and ADFS): rooms, scenarios and the front end. Not checked: real hardware, MOS 3.50, other ROMs that claim HAZEL. |
| 17 | 2026-10-04 | **No joystick** | Matt's call. The original merged a Kempston joystick with the keys; the BBC version is keys only (redefinable from the menu). MENU's row 23, which said JOYSTICK COMPATIBLE, offers the instructions instead. |
| 18 | 2026-10-04 | **Harry is white in the 13 rooms where white shows 2 points more of the room in its own colour** (revises 6) | Matt: "white sparingly when it works better". Forcing yellow into every palette turned much of the original's white yellow (room 5's lattice, room 48's pipes). In rooms 5, 48, 100, 92, 83, 82, 43, 104, 93, 52, 51, 4 and 94 logical 3 is white, and Spectrum yellow is pinned to it in every band, so Harry (drawn in Spectrum yellow) is white there and stays one colour across splits. The cost: anything yellow in those rooms is white too (room 5's bird, room 92's kettle, room 104's lift); some white things that showed yellow come right. 99.03% of pixels in their own colour becomes 99.53%, with 75 splits instead of 93. Explored by an agent: scratchpad report with screenshots, cut-offs from 0.5 to 2 points compared. |
| 19 | 2026-10-04 | **More of the machine for the game** (refines 11): the stack keeps only `&01C0-&01FF`, with tables below it in page 1; the OS vectors other than IRQ1V hold tables too; LowState's most used variables move into zero page `&28-&8E` (baron's pool shrinks to the 40 bytes it uses); the palette bands' variables move from sideways RAM to LowState; the start-up code runs in the discarded loader | Measured, not assumed: the deepest stack in play is `&01EA` (22 bytes, the interrupt's included, over every room, deaths and random play; a static bound from the listing gives 28); the MOS's IRQ entry reads only IRQ1V and `&FC` (OS 1.20 and MOS 3.20, read in jsbeeb), the game makes no OS calls, and a reset puts the vectors back. Zero page saves a byte on every access. Main RAM 134 bytes free becoming 886, sideways RAM 34 becoming 116. The interrupt no longer reads sideways RAM. Behaviour unchanged: rooms, scenarios and the front end pass on both machines. |
| 20 | 2026-10-04 | **Sprite frames are stored a column at a time**: the original's frames, byte for byte, with each frame's rows turned into columns (`tools/mkgfx.py`, `tools/mkobjects.py`) | Speed: the drawing, erasing and collision test work down a frame a column at a time, with Y the line in the cell indexing the screen, the tile and the frame together. Stored by rows, the frame's pointer had to step on width - 1 bytes a line, 14 cycles a byte drawn or erased; by columns Y alone steps. It saved about 4,000 cycles in the slowest stretch measured (room 30's two monsters), and the collision test reads Harry's two columns directly. The frames don't overlap, and nothing else reads their pixels (the machines' strips are one byte wide, the same either way). |
| 21 | 2026-10-04 | **The room and front-end data packed tighter, and the tile font in sideways RAM** (refines 5, 8, 15): the 26 different palettes of the 120 rooms are kept once each, a room's number for its palette in its packed first byte beside the paper (so that byte no longer unpacks to the original's attribute, of which the drawer only ever used the paper); bands name one of 19 shared colour maps; packed rooms are found by adding up their lengths; rows, run lengths and the ends of capped runs are tier coded; MENU's high-score table keeps two digits to a byte; the tile font moves to the bank | Room for features: main RAM 2,868 bytes free becoming 3,762, sideways 188 becoming 122. Nothing the game does changes: every room unpacks and draws as before (120 of 120 against the original), the palettes and bands set for every room are byte for byte as before, so are the screens over 30 passes in 95 rooms, and the game is as fast (tools/perf.mjs the same in every room). Drawing a room takes 0.8% longer (the tier codes and the lengths added up). |
| 22 | 2026-10-04 | **BREAK in play comes back to MENU** | Matt asked for it, though "lots of games left the machine in a weird state on a soft break". The game no longer puts anything where a soft BREAK looks: page 2 beyond the vectors (`&0258`, `&027F-&029B`, the ROM table, a Master's EXEC and SPOOL handles), page 3 (a Master's screen mode in `&0355`), zero page `&D0` (a Master's VDU status) and `&0D9F-&0DFF` (a Master's extended vectors). `thing_types`, the monster types, the death tune's notes and the band variables moved to main RAM (526 bytes, affordable since decisions 19-21); page 1 below the stack and page 2's vectors keep their tables. The start-up option that makes BREAK boot without SHIFT is flipped when the game starts, not just at its own reset, and MENU puts back the value the game saved (not a flip back, in case a CTRL-BREAK has read the keyboard links since). Checked on the Model B and on jsbeeb's Master with DFS and with ADFS: the menu comes back, without the instructions, the option restored, and the game plays again. |
| 23 | 2026-10-04 | **Saves and loads ask for a filename, below the disc's catalogue** (refines 13) | Matt: "we need to pick a load and save filename ... and add an option to the load menu to list the disc". The original saves to tape with no name and no prompt. MENU's save and load screens list the disc (`*CAT`) and ask for a name on row 21, offering the last one used (kept in the mailbox, so it outlasts games, BREAK and CTRL-BREAK; "CEGAME" at first). RETURN takes it; ESCAPE goes back: to the game, from the save key in play (the original's SPACE abort abandons the game, but no keypress here should cost a game); to the menu otherwise. A disc error ("Bad name", "Disc full", ...) shows on row 24 and the name is asked for again, where it used to drop back to the menu. Only a file a saved game's length is loaded (`OSFILE 5` first: "NOT FOUND", "NOT A SAVED GAME"), so loading `CE2` can't run over the screen into the sideways bank, and a save won't overwrite a file that isn't one, so a mistyped `MENU` can't spoil the disc. |
| 24 | 2026-10-04 | **No split where an object crossing it would change colour** (refines 15, revises 18's figures) | Matt: room 1's ladder was red above its split and blue below, "it should be the same colour throughout". tools/mkrooms.py now counts, at every boundary between rows, the pixels of a colour (not the paper) with the same colour just across it, and won't split there if any of them would change colour. 26 rooms had such cuts (125 pixels in all). With the rule: 49 splits instead of 75, 99.48% of pixels in their own colour instead of 99.53%, 14 shared colour maps instead of 19 (72 bytes of sideways RAM back), and Harry white in 18 rooms (still 2 points or more each: 5, 51, 92, 83, 48, 39, 82, 93, 100, 86, 104, 24, 95, 53, 52, 59, 4, 94). Room 1's ladder is blue top to bottom. |
| 25 | 2026-10-04 | **BBC keys by default: Z and X left and right, : and / up and down, RETURN jump, TAB take/drop, R abort, S save** (revises 7) | Matt's choice, after playing: the classic BBC layout instead of the Spectrum's Q/A/O/P, SPACE, 1, 0, S. | 26 | 2026-10-04 | **Sprite frames packed** (refines 20; `tools/mkgfx.py`, `tools/mkobjects.py`): a monster's, a lift's or an object's frame leaves out its empty top and bottom lines, how many in the header bytes' spare bits (the height byte's bits 3-7, the width byte's 4-6), which the few readers of a frame's size mask off; a frame that is another's mirror image is a 4-byte stub (bit 7 of the width byte, then where its twin's pixels are), drawn turned over; the 16 bytes no pointer reaches are dropped. Harry's frames (the collision test reads his pixels) and the truck's and train's strips (painted, empty lines and all) stay whole | Room in the bank: the sprite frames' 7,766 bytes become 6,339 (772 of empty lines, 639 of 15 mirror images, 16 unreachable), so sideways RAM has 1,601 bytes free, not 174. The objects' 1,256 become 1,157 in main RAM (93 of lines, one mirror image). The code costs 220 bytes of main RAM (3,010 free becoming 2,889). A mirror's columns are its twin's walked the other way, the screen halves swapped and each nibble spread through a turned-over table, the same work a byte as any frame; `sprite_mode` patches draw_sprite and erase_image only when the way changes. Drawing an empty line changes nothing, so every screen is the same (`tools/screencmp.mjs`, idle and moving, 95 rooms), and the slowest stretch between VSync waits is a little shorter: 24,400 cycles idle (25,700 before), 27,800 moving (28,500). |
| 27 | 2026-10-04 | **The original's developers' cheats, behind f0 on the menu** | Matt: "let's put the cheat mode back in! Much more authentic ... a hidden keypress in MENU". The original reads a cheat byte from `&FFFF`, outside the tape image, so only a specially made tape set it (research/frontend.md): `%101` then bit 4 infinite lives (the death's `DEC` of the lives made a NOP, which also leaves the game-over test never taken), bit 3 the room skip (CAPS SHIFT and left or right: -1, +1, or -3 with both, wrapping 1-120, the room entered as if walked into), bits 0-2 the starting egg - 1; anything else showed PLEASE TRY AGAIN and reset. Here f0 on the menu, mentioned nowhere on screen, turns on the room skip and infinite lives (`%10111000`) and puts a flashing banner between the high scores and the menu; while they're on, f1-f8 choose the starting egg. SHIFT stands in for CAPS SHIFT. The byte goes in the mailbox and is saved with a game, as `&A3FC` was; a load applies the saved byte's cheats and the menu's together, as the Spectrum's patches were never undone. The game tests the byte where the original patched its code. Left out: PLEASE TRY AGAIN (MENU only makes good bytes). Changed: -3 from rooms 1 and 2 gave rooms 254 and 255 on the Spectrum, past the room table; here they wrap to 118 and 119. Checked pass by pass against the original with the same cheats applied (scenarios cheat-skip and cheat-lives). |
